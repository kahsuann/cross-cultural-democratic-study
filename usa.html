USA, A Teacher's Perspective

I am Alex, a 40-year-old middle school teacher with 2 kids aged 7 and 11. I can still remember two months ago when news broke about the assassination of Charlie Kirk, an American right-wing activist who was killed while speaking at a public debate in Utah Valley University. In its aftermath, the school called for a PTA meeting to discuss a new proposal - allowing trained staff to carry concealed firearms on campus.

Leading up to the meeting, the teachers were split on the proposal. One of my colleagues said that she signed up to be a teacher and not a cop and expressed her fear of having weapons in her workplace. Another staff member then rebutted saying: "We are sitting ducks in here. If someone comes in with a gun, what do we do? We have the right to protect the kids and ourselves!"

The meeting officially started in the school auditorium. One could easily tell which side was supportive of the proposal. On one side, parents donned shirts showing their support for the Second Amendment while those sitting on the other side wore pins of disapproval. As a teacher and parent, I felt straddled between both sides but I knew I had to speak up to have my views heard.

“I know that for many people, owning a gun isn’t about wanting violence,” I said. “It’s about being able to defend your family and property. I also stand in front of my students having to explain why we need to have lockdown drills and having open discussions about gun control. As a parent, I wish for my kid to return home safe every day. But I think the responsibility of keeping our kids safe should not resort to having staff carry concealed weapons in school. We are educators, not the police.”

In this day and age, it feels worrisome that one’s job could be at risk if we state our opinion online and it strikes the wrong chord with others. I have seen fellow educators having to resign after showing their disdain for the bearing of arms. Therefore, instead of penning down my views online, I engage with my representative and write to them to let them know about what I feel about certain policies, hoping to have my voice heard by them.

I think that everyone is entitled to their freedom of speech as per our First Amendment. No one should be punished for sharing their views. On the contentious Second Amendment act, I believe keeping an open communication is the key to moving forward.
