Reflections

  • Across these stories, we see that democracy is not a fixed, universal template but a set of evolving practices shaped by culture, history, and lived experience.
  • What counts as "participation" - protest, voting, dialogue or trust - varies across societies, and so does the meaning of equality, voice, and collective responsibility.
  • These differences raise a deeper question: Should democracy look the same everywhere? Or should each society define it according to its own values and concerns?

Food for Thought Questions

  • Is there a single "correct" form of democracy, or should democratic practices adapt to cultural contexts?
  • Which character's understanding of democracy resonated most with you? Why?
  • Should democracy prioritise individual freedom, collective harmony, or consensus? Can these values coexist?
